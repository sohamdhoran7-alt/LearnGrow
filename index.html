Chapter 1: Rotational Dynamics

📖 Core Definitions & Concepts

1. Circular Motion
The motion of an object along the circumference of a circle is called circular motion.

Examples: merry-go-round, vehicle taking a turn, satellite around a planet.
2. Characteristics of Circular Motion
• It is accelerated motion because the direction of velocity continuously changes.
• It is periodic motion because the particle repeats the same path.
3. Radius Vector
A vector describing the position of a particle performing circular motion with respect to the centre of the circle.
4. Angular Displacement
The angle described by the radius vector in a given time at the centre of the circle.

SI unit: radian
It is dimensionless.
θ = s / r
s = rθ
5. Angular Velocity
Angular velocity is the rate of change of angular displacement with respect to time.

SI unit: rad/s
It is a vector quantity.
ω = Δθ / Δt

v = rω
6. Angular Acceleration
Angular acceleration is the rate of change of angular velocity with respect to time.

SI unit: rad/s²
α = Δω / Δt
7. Uniform Circular Motion
Motion of a particle along the circumference of a circle with constant speed.
8. Non-Uniform Circular Motion
Motion of a particle along the circumference of a circle with variable speed.

Centripetal and Centrifugal Force

9. Centripetal Force
The force acting on a particle performing circular motion and directed towards the centre is called centripetal force.
ac = v²/r = rω² = vω

Fc = mv²/r = mrω² = mvω
10. Centrifugal Force
The apparent force acting away from the centre during circular motion is called centrifugal force. It is a pseudo force.

Its magnitude is equal to the centripetal force.

Vehicle on Horizontal Circular Track

For a vehicle moving on a level circular road, static friction provides the centripetal force.
mv²/r = μmg

vmax = √(μrg)

Banking of Road

Banking of road means raising the outer edge of a road above its inner edge through a certain angle.
Angle of banking is the angle made by the surface of the road with the horizontal.

Maximum Safety Speed with Friction

vmax = √[rg(tanθ + μs) / (1 − μstanθ)]

Minimum Safety Speed with Friction

vmin = √[rg(tanθ − μs) / (1 + μstanθ)]

Most Safe Speed

v = √(rg tanθ)

θ = tan⁻¹(v²/rg)

Conical Pendulum

A conical pendulum consists of a bob moving in a horizontal circle while the string makes an angle θ with the vertical.
T₀ cosθ = mg
T₀ sinθ = mrω²
ω = √[g/(L cosθ)]
T = 2π√(L cosθ/g)

Vertical Circular Motion

Particle on a string: At the topmost position, the minimum speed required to just complete the circle is:
vA,min = √(rg)
vB,min = √(5rg)

vD = √(3rg)
For vertical circular motion using a rigid rod:
vA,min = 0
vB,min = 2√(rg)

Rigid Body, Axis, Torque and Couple

Rigid Body: A body whose geometrical shape and size remain unchanged under an external force.

Axis of Rotation: A straight line about which the particles of a rigid body move in circles.

Torque: The turning effect of a force about an axis.

Couple: Two equal and opposite forces acting at different points of a body and producing rotational motion.

Moment of Inertia

Moment of inertia is the sum of the products of the mass of each particle and the square of its perpendicular distance from the axis of rotation.
I = Σmᵢrᵢ²

I = ∫r²dm
SI unit: kg m²
Dimensions: [ML²T⁰]

Factors Affecting Moment of Inertia

1. Mass of the body
2. Shape and size of the body
3. Distribution of mass about the axis
4. Position and orientation of the axis

Moment of Inertia of Ring

I = MR²

Moment of Inertia of Disc

I = ½MR²

Angular Momentum

Angular momentum of a rotating body is the product of its moment of inertia and angular velocity.
L = Iω

Torque in Terms of Moment of Inertia

τ = Iα

Rotational Kinetic Energy

The kinetic energy of a rotating body is:
K = ½Iω²

Theorem of Perpendicular Axes

For a plane lamina, the moment of inertia about an axis perpendicular to its plane is equal to the sum of the moments of inertia about two mutually perpendicular axes lying in its plane.
Iz = Ix + Iy

Theorem of Parallel Axes

The moment of inertia about any axis is equal to the moment of inertia about a parallel axis passing through the centre of mass plus Mh².
IO = IC + Mh²

Conservation of Angular Momentum

If the net external torque acting on a body is zero, its angular momentum remains constant.
dL/dt = τ

If τ = 0, then L = constant.
Examples include ballet dancers, skaters, divers and acrobats.

Radius of Gyration

Radius of gyration is the distance from the axis at which the whole mass of a body may be assumed to be concentrated without changing its moment of inertia.
I = MK²

K = √(I/M)
SI unit: metre
Dimension: [L]

Rolling Motion

Rolling motion is a combination of pure translational and pure rotational motion without slipping.
KEroll = ½Mv² + ½Iω²

ω = v/R
KEroll = ½Mv²(1 + K²/R²)

Rolling Down an Inclined Plane

v = √[2gh/(1 + K²/R²)]

a = g sinθ/(1 + K²/R²)

Rotational Kinematic Equations

ω = ω₀ + αt

θ = ω₀t + ½αt²

ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ

Translational Equations

v = u + at

s = ut + ½at²

v² = u² + 2as

📝 Practice MCQs

1. The SI unit of moment of inertia is:
A) kg m
B) kg m²
C) N m
D) J

Answer: B) kg m²
2. Angular momentum is given by:
A) mv
B) Iω
C) ma
D) Iα

Answer: B) Iω
3. Rotational kinetic energy is:
A) ½mv²
B) mgh
C) ½Iω²
D) Fd

Answer: C) ½Iω²
4. The rotational equivalent of mass is:
A) Torque
B) Angular velocity
C) Moment of inertia
D) Angular acceleration

Answer: C) Moment of inertia
5. If external torque is zero, which quantity remains conserved?
A) Force
B) Linear velocity
C) Angular momentum
D) Acceleration

Answer: C) Angular momentum
