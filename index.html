Chapter 1: Rotational Dynamics

📖 Core Definitions & Concepts

1. Circular Motion
The motion of an object along the circumference of a circle is called circular motion.

Examples: merry-go-round, vehicle taking a turn, satellite around a planet.
2. Characteristics of Circular Motion
• It is accelerated motion because the direction of velocity continuously changes.
• It is periodic motion because the particle repeats the same path.
3. Radius Vector
A vector describing the position of a particle performing circular motion with respect to the centre of the circle.
4. Angular Displacement
The angle described by the radius vector in a given time at the centre of the circle.

SI unit: radian (dimensionless)
θ = s / r   |   s = rθ
5. Angular Velocity
Rate of change of angular displacement with respect to time.
SI unit: rad/s (vector)
ω = Δθ / Δt   |   v = rω
6. Angular Acceleration
Rate of change of angular velocity with respect to time.
SI unit: rad/s²
α = Δω / Δt
7. Uniform Circular Motion
Motion along the circumference of a circle with constant speed.
8. Non-Uniform Circular Motion
Motion along the circumference of a circle with variable speed.

Centripetal and Centrifugal Force

9. Centripetal Force
Force acting on a particle performing circular motion and directed towards the centre.
ac = v²/r = rω² = vω
Fc = mv²/r = mrω² = mvω
10. Centrifugal Force
Apparent force acting away from the centre during circular motion (pseudo force). Magnitude equal to centripetal force.

Vehicle on Horizontal Circular Track

Static friction provides the centripetal force.
mv²/r = μmg   →   vmax = √(μrg)

Banking of Road

Banking of road – raising the outer edge of a road above its inner edge through a certain angle.
Angle of banking – angle made by the road surface with the horizontal.

Maximum Safety Speed (with friction)

vmax = √[rg(tanθ + μs) / (1 − μstanθ)]

Minimum Safety Speed (with friction)

vmin = √[rg(tanθ − μs) / (1 + μstanθ)]

Most Safe Speed (no friction)

v = √(rg tanθ)   |   θ = tan⁻¹(v²/rg)

Conical Pendulum

Bob moves in a horizontal circle while the string makes angle θ with the vertical.
T₀ cosθ = mg
T₀ sinθ = mrω²
ω = √[g/(L cosθ)]
T = 2π√(L cosθ/g)

Vertical Circular Motion

Particle on a string – minimum speeds:
vA,min (top) = √(rg)
vB,min (bottom) = √(5rg)
vD (horizontal) = √(3rg)
For rigid rod:
vA,min = 0   |   vB,min = 2√(rg)

Rigid Body, Axis, Torque and Couple

Rigid Body: geometrical shape and size remain unchanged under external force.
Axis of Rotation: straight line about which particles move in circles.
Torque: turning effect of a force about an axis.
Couple: two equal and opposite forces acting at different points producing rotation.

Moment of Inertia

Sum of products of mass of each particle and square of its perpendicular distance from the axis.
I = Σ mᵢ rᵢ² = ∫ r² dm
SI unit: kg m²    Dimensions: [ML²T⁰]
Factors affecting I: mass, shape & size, distribution of mass, position & orientation of axis.

Moment of Inertia of Ring

I = MR²

Moment of Inertia of Disc

I = ½ MR²

Angular Momentum

L = Iω

Torque in Terms of Moment of Inertia

τ = Iα

Rotational Kinetic Energy

K = ½ I ω²

Theorem of Perpendicular Axes

For a plane lamina: Iz = Ix + Iy

Theorem of Parallel Axes

IO = IC + Mh²

Conservation of Angular Momentum

If net external torque is zero, angular momentum remains constant.
dL/dt = τ   →   if τ = 0 then L = constant
Examples: ballet dancers, skaters, divers, acrobats.

Radius of Gyration

Distance from the axis at which the whole mass may be assumed concentrated without changing I.
I = MK²   |   K = √(I/M)
SI unit: m    Dimension: [L]

Rolling Motion

Combination of pure translation and pure rotation without slipping.
KEroll = ½ Mv² + ½ I ω² = ½ Mv² (1 + K²/R²)
ω = v/R

Rolling Down an Inclined Plane

v = √[2gh / (1 + K²/R²)]
a = g sinθ / (1 + K²/R²)

Rotational Kinematic Equations

ω = ω₀ + αt
θ = ω₀ t + ½ α t²
ω² = ω₀² + 2 α θ

Chapter 2: Mechanical Properties of Fluids

HSC Physics | Maharashtra Board

Fluid

A fluid is a substance that can flow. Liquids and gases are common examples.

Ideal Fluid

  • Incompressible
  • Non-viscous
  • Irrotational
  • Steady flow

Pressure

Pressure is the normal force acting per unit area.

P = F / A

SI unit: Pascal (Pa)  |  Dimensions: [ML⁻¹T⁻²]

Pressure Due to Liquid Column

P = hρg

h = depth, ρ = density, g = acceleration due to gravity

Pascal's Law

Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally and undiminished in all directions.

Applications

  • Hydraulic lift
  • Hydraulic brakes

Atmospheric Pressure

Pressure produced by the weight of the Earth's atmosphere.

Gauge Pressure

Gauge Pressure = Absolute Pressure − Atmospheric Pressure

Surface Tension

Tangential force acting per unit length on the free surface of a liquid.

T = F / L

SI unit: N/m

Surface Energy

Work required to increase the surface area of a liquid surface.

Angle of Contact

Angle between the tangent to the liquid surface and the solid surface at the point of contact.

Laplace's Law

For a liquid drop:

ΔP = 2T / r

For a soap bubble:

ΔP = 4T / r

Capillarity

The rise or fall of a liquid in a narrow tube is called capillarity.

Examples

  • Oil rising through a lamp wick
  • Water movement through blotting paper
  • Water movement through soil
  • Rise of water in plants

Capillary Rise

h = 2T cosθ / (rρg)

Hydrodynamics

Study of fluids in motion.

Streamline Flow

Fluid particles move smoothly in an orderly manner.

Turbulent Flow

Irregular flow in which velocity can change with time and eddies may form.

Reynolds Number

Rₙ = ρvd / η

Viscosity

Property of a fluid that opposes relative motion between its layers.

Newton's Law of Viscosity

F = η A (dv/dx)

SI unit of coefficient of viscosity: Pa·s

Stokes' Law

Fᵥ = 6πηrv

Terminal Velocity

Constant velocity reached by a falling body when the forces acting on it become balanced.

vₜ = 2r² (ρ − σ)g / 9η

Equation of Continuity

A₁v₁ = A₂v₂   |   Av = Constant

Important Formulas

P = F/A
P = hρg
T = F/L
ΔP = 2T/r  (drop)  |  ΔP = 4T/r  (soap bubble)
h = 2T cosθ / (rρg)
Rₙ = ρvd / η
Fᵥ = 6πηrv
vₜ = 2r²(ρ−σ)g / 9η
A₁v₁ = A₂v₂

Quick Revision

  • Fluid → substance that can flow.
  • Pressure → force per unit area.
  • Pascal's law → pressure is transmitted equally in an enclosed fluid.
  • Surface tension → force per unit length.
  • Capillarity → rise or fall of liquid in a narrow tube.
  • Viscosity → resistance between fluid layers.
  • Stokes' law → Fᵥ = 6πηrv.
  • Continuity equation → A₁v₁ = A₂v₂.
