Chapter 1: Rotational Dynamics

📖 Core Definitions & Concepts

1. Circular Motion
The motion of an object along the circumference of a circle is called circular motion.

Examples: merry-go-round, vehicle taking a turn, satellite around a planet.
2. Characteristics of Circular Motion
• It is accelerated motion because the direction of velocity continuously changes.
• It is periodic motion because the particle repeats the same path.
3. Radius Vector
A vector describing the position of a particle performing circular motion with respect to the centre of the circle.
4. Angular Displacement
The angle described by the radius vector in a given time at the centre of the circle.

SI unit: radian
It is dimensionless.
θ = s / r

s = rθ
5. Angular Velocity
Angular velocity is the rate of change of angular displacement with respect to time.

SI unit: rad/s
It is a vector quantity.
ω = Δθ / Δt

v = rω
6. Angular Acceleration
Angular acceleration is the rate of change of angular velocity with respect to time.

SI unit: rad/s²
α = Δω / Δt
7. Uniform Circular Motion
Motion of a particle along the circumference of a circle with constant speed.
8. Non-Uniform Circular Motion
Motion of a particle along the circumference of a circle with variable speed.

Centripetal and Centrifugal Force

9. Centripetal Force
The force acting on a particle performing circular motion and directed towards the centre is called centripetal force.
ac = v²/r = rω²

Fc = mv²/r = mrω²
10. Centrifugal Force
The apparent force acting away from the centre during circular motion is called centrifugal force.

It is a pseudo force.

Vehicle on Horizontal Circular Track

For a vehicle moving on a level circular road, static friction provides the centripetal force.
mv²/r = μmg

vmax = √(μrg)

Banking of Road

Banking of road means raising the outer edge of a road above its inner edge through a certain angle.
v = √(rg tanθ)

θ = tan⁻¹(v²/rg)

Moment of Inertia

Moment of inertia is the sum of the products of the mass of each particle and the square of its perpendicular distance from the axis of rotation.
I = Σmᵢrᵢ²

I = ∫r²dm
SI unit: kg m²
Dimensions: [ML²T⁰]

Angular Momentum

L = Iω

Torque

τ = Iα

Rotational Kinetic Energy

K = ½Iω²

Conservation of Angular Momentum

If the net external torque acting on a body is zero, its angular momentum remains constant.
dL/dt = τ

If τ = 0, then L = constant.

Radius of Gyration

I = MK²

K = √(I/M)

Rolling Motion

KEroll = ½Mv² + ½Iω²

ω = v/R

Rotational Kinematic Equations

ω = ω₀ + αt

θ = ω₀t + ½αt²

ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ

Chapter 2: Mechanical Properties of Fluids

HSC Physics | Maharashtra Board

Fluid

A fluid is a substance that can flow. Liquids and gases are common examples.

Ideal Fluid

  • Incompressible
  • Non-viscous
  • Irrotational
  • Steady flow

Pressure

Pressure is the normal force acting per unit area.

P = F / A

SI unit: Pascal (Pa)

Dimensions: [ML⁻¹T⁻²]

Pressure Due to Liquid Column

P = hρg

Here h is depth, ρ is density and g is acceleration due to gravity.

Pascal's Law

Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally and undiminished in all directions.

Applications

  • Hydraulic lift
  • Hydraulic brakes

Atmospheric Pressure

Atmospheric pressure is the pressure produced by the weight of the Earth's atmosphere.

Gauge Pressure

Gauge Pressure = Absolute Pressure − Atmospheric Pressure

Surface Tension

Surface tension is the tangential force acting per unit length on the free surface of a liquid.

T = F / L

SI unit: N/m

Surface Energy

Surface energy is the work required to increase the surface area of a liquid surface.

Angle of Contact

It is the angle between the tangent to the liquid surface and the solid surface at the point of contact.

Laplace's Law

For a liquid drop:

ΔP = 2T / r

For a soap bubble:

ΔP = 4T / r

Capillarity

The rise or fall of a liquid in a narrow tube is called capillarity.

Examples

  • Oil rising through a lamp wick
  • Water movement through blotting paper
  • Water movement through soil
  • Rise of water in plants

Capillary Rise

h = 2T cosθ / (rρg)

Hydrodynamics

Hydrodynamics is the study of fluids in motion.

Streamline Flow

In streamline flow, fluid particles move smoothly in an orderly manner.

Turbulent Flow

Turbulent flow is irregular flow in which velocity can change with time and eddies may form.

Reynolds Number

Rₑ = ρvd / η

Viscosity

Viscosity is the property of a fluid that opposes relative motion between its layers.

Newton's Law of Viscosity

F = ηA(dv/dx)

SI unit of coefficient of viscosity: Pa·s

Stokes' Law

Fᵥ = 6πηrv

Terminal Velocity

The constant velocity reached by a falling body when the forces acting on it become balanced is called terminal velocity.

vₜ = 2r²(ρ − σ)g / 9η

Equation of Continuity

A₁v₁ = A₂v₂
Av = Constant

Quick Revision

  • Fluid → substance that can flow.
  • Pressure → force per unit area.
  • Pascal's law → pressure is transmitted equally in an enclosed fluid.
  • Surface tension → force per unit length.
  • Capillarity → rise or fall of liquid in a narrow tube.
  • Viscosity → resistance between fluid layers.
  • Stokes' law → Fᵥ = 6πηrv.
  • Continuity equation → A₁v₁ = A₂v₂.

Chapter 3: Kinetic Theory of Gases and Radiation

HSC Physics | Maharashtra Board

Q1. What are the modes of transfer of heat energy?

Ans: Heat energy flows from a region of higher temperature to a region of lower temperature. There are three modes of transfer of heat:

1. Conduction
Transfer of heat through a material medium without actual movement of the particles of the medium.

2. Convection
Transfer of heat through a fluid by actual movement of its particles.

3. Radiation
Transfer of heat in the form of electromagnetic waves without requiring a material medium. Radiation can travel through vacuum.
Q2. Explain the concept of an ideal gas.

Ans: An ideal gas is a gas which satisfies the following conditions:

• The size of gas molecules is negligibly small.
• There is negligible intermolecular force of attraction.
• It obeys gas laws strictly.

Boyle's Law: P ∝ 1/V at constant temperature.

Charles' Law: V ∝ T at constant pressure.

Gay-Lussac's Law: P ∝ T at constant volume.

PV = nRT
Q3. What is Equation of State?

Ans: The equation relating physical quantities such as pressure P, temperature T and volume V of a gas is called the equation of state.
Q4. Write the assumptions of kinetic theory of gases.

Ans:
• Gas consists of a large number of very small molecules.
• Molecules are identical for the same gas.
• Molecules are in continuous random motion.
• Collisions between molecules and container walls are perfectly elastic.
• Molecules move in straight lines between collisions.
• Intermolecular forces are negligible.
• Actual volume of molecules is very small compared with the gas volume.
• Average kinetic energy of molecules is proportional to absolute temperature.
Q5. Derive an expression for average pressure of a gas.

Ans: From kinetic theory of gases:
P = 1/3 ρ v²
where ρ is the density of the gas and v² is the mean square velocity.
Q6. Obtain the expression for RMS velocity.

Ans:
vrms = √(3P/ρ)
For an ideal gas:
vrms = √(3RT/M)
Q7. Obtain the expression for kinetic energy.

Ans:
E = 3/2 PV
For an ideal gas:
E = 3/2 NkBT
Therefore, average kinetic energy per molecule is:
E/N = 3/2 kBT
Q8. Define Degree of Freedom.

Ans: The number of independent coordinates required to describe the position and configuration of a system completely is called the degree of freedom.

For a monoatomic gas, degree of freedom = 3.

For a diatomic gas, degree of freedom = 5.
Q9. Explain Specific Heat.

Ans: The amount of heat required to raise the temperature of unit mass of a substance by 1°C is called its specific heat.

For gases, two specific heats are considered:
• CV = specific heat at constant volume
• CP = specific heat at constant pressure
Q10. Mayer's Relation

For one mole of an ideal gas:
CP − CV = R
This is known as Mayer's relation.
Q11. Define Diathermanous body.

Ans: A body which allows heat radiation to pass through it is called a diathermanous body.
Q12. Define Athermanous substance.

Ans: A body which does not allow heat radiation to pass through it is called an athermanous substance.
Q13. Define coefficient of absorption.

Ans: The ratio of radiant heat absorbed by a body to the total radiant heat incident on it is called coefficient of absorption.
a = Qa/Q
Q14. Define coefficient of reflection.

Ans: The ratio of radiant heat reflected by a body to the total radiant heat incident on it is called coefficient of reflection.
r = Qr/Q
Q15. Define coefficient of transmission.

Ans: The ratio of radiant heat transmitted through a body to the total radiant heat incident on it is called coefficient of transmission.
t = Qt/Q
Q16. Obtain the relation between a, r and t.

The incident radiant heat is divided into absorbed, reflected and transmitted heat.
Q = Qa + Qr + Qt
Therefore:
a + r + t = 1
Q17. What is a perfect black body?

Ans: A body which absorbs all the radiant energy incident upon it is called a perfect black body.
a = 1
For a perfect black body:
r = 0, t = 0
