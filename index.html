Q1 What are the modes of transfer of heat energy?

Ans: The heat energy flows from one point to the another point due to the temperature difference between them. It tends to move from the high temperature point (region) to the low temperature point (region). There are three modes (methods) of transfer of heat.

Conduction
Convection
Radiation

Conduction:- It is the mode of transfer of heat through the material medium without actual migration of the particles of the medium from one place to another is called conduction. In solids, the heat is transfer by conduction mainly or conduction material medium is necessary & it cannot pass through vacuum.

Convection:- It is the mode of transfer of heat through material medium by the actual migration of particles of the medium is called convection. In fluids (liquid and gases) the heat is transfer by convection.

Radiation:- In radiation, the heat is transferred from a body at higher temperature to a body at lower temperature in the form of electro-magnetic waves without affecting the intermediate medium. No material medium is necessary for radiation. It means that it can travel through vacuum.
Q2 Explain the concept of an ideal gas

Ans: Ideal gas is that gas which full fill the following condition.

The size of the gas molecule is negligibly small.

Gas molecule are widely separated therefore, there is no intermolecular force of attraction between the molecules of the gas.

Ideal gas strictly obeys all the gas laws.

a. Boyle’s law: \( P \propto 1/V \) at constant \( T \) --- (3.1)
b. Charles’s law: \( V \propto T \) at constant \( P \) --- (3.2)
c. Gay-Lussac’s law: \( P \propto T \) at constant \( V \) --- (3.3)

Ideal gases satisfy following relation between pressure, volume and temperature of the gas for any value of \( P, V \) and \( T \). By combining them relation is given by

PV = nRT
Where \( P \) = pressure of gas
\( V \) = volume of the gas
\( n \) = no. of moles
\( R \) = gas constant
\( T \) = temperature of gas
Q3 What is Equation of State?

Ans: Equation of State: For a gas, its state is specified by a number of physical quantities such as pressure \( P \), temperature \( T \), volume \( V \), internal energy \( E \), etc. Hence, the equation relating these quantities is known as the equation of state.
Q4 Write the Assumptions of kinetic theory of gases

Ans: Assumptions:

Gas consists of large number of extremely small particles called as gas molecules.

All molecules of same gas are identical.

Molecules are rigid & perfectly elastic spheres.

The gas molecules are in the state of random motion i.e. they are moving continuously in all possible direction with all possible velocities.

During random motion, they collide with other molecules and with the wall of the container. These collisions are perfectly elastic collisions.

A molecule travels in a straight line with uniform speed between two successive collisions.

The time taken for the collision is very small as compared to the time interval between two successive collisions.

The average number of molecules per unit volume of the gas is constant.

The inter-molecular force of attraction between the two molecules is negligible.

The actual volume occupied by the gas molecules is very small as compared to the total volume of the gas.

The average KE of the gas molecule is directly proportional to the absolute temperature of the gas.

The distance covered by the molecule between two successive collisions is called free path.

The average distance covered by the molecule between two successive collisions is called mean free path.

NOTE
Mean free path is measures in Angstrom unit or meter.

Remember this:
Higher the density, more will be the collisions and smaller will be the mean free path \( \lambda \). It is also seen that \( \lambda \) is inversely proportional to the size of the molecule, say the diameter \( d \). Smaller the size of the molecule, less is the chance for collision and larger is the mean free path. Further, \( \lambda \) is inversely proportional to \( d^2 \), not just \( d \), because it depends on the cross section of a molecule. It can be shown that \( \lambda = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} \pi d^2 (N/V)} \)

Let \( \lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3, \dots \lambda_N \) be the free paths then

\( \lambda = \frac{\lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 + \dots + \lambda_N}{N} \)
Q5 Derive an expression for Average pressure of gas using kinetic theory of gases and radiation

Ans: Consider an ideal gas enclosed in a cubical vessel of length \( L \). Suppose there are \( N \) molecules in a gas which are moving with velocities \( v \).

If we consider any single molecule than its instantaneous velocity \( v \) can be expressed as

\( v = v_x i + v_y j + v_z k \)

Due to random motion of the molecule \( v_x = v_y = v_z \)

Suppose a molecule 1 of mass \( m \) is moving with a velocity \( v_x \) towards the face ABCD. It strikes the face of the cubical vessel and returns back to strike the opposite face.

Change in Momentum molecule = Final Momentum – Initial Momentum

\( \Delta p = -m v_x - m v_x = -2 m v_x \)

Momentum transferred to the wall of the vessel per molecule per collision \( P = 2 m v_x \)

The distance travelled by the molecule in going to face ABCD and coming back is \( 2L \). So, the time between two successive collision is \( \Delta t = \frac{2L}{v_x} \)

Force exerted on the shaded wall by molecule 1 is

Average force = Average rate of change of momentum = \( \frac{2 m v_x}{\Delta t} = \frac{2 m v_x}{2L / v_x} = \frac{m v_x^2}{L} \)

Considering other molecules 2, 3, 4 ... with the respective x components of velocities \( v_{x2}, v_{x3}, v_{x4} \dots \) the total average force on the wall is given by

\( F_x = \frac{m}{L} (v_{x1}^2 + v_{x2}^2 + v_{x3}^2 + \dots) \)

Average pressure = \( \frac{\text{Average Force}}{\text{Area of Shaded wall}} \)

= \( \frac{m (v_{x1}^2 + v_{x2}^2 + v_{x3}^2 + \dots)}{L \cdot L^2} \) [∴ Area = \( L^2 \)]

The average velocity \( \overline{v_x^2} = \frac{(v_{x1}^2 + v_{x2}^2 + v_{x3}^2 + \dots)}{N} \)

∴ \( P = \frac{m N \overline{v_x^2}}{V} \)

As \( v_x = v_y = v_z \) ∴ \( \overline{v_x^2} = \frac{1}{3} \overline{v^2} \)

\( \overline{v^2} = \overline{v_x^2} + \overline{v_y^2} + \overline{v_z^2} \)

∴ \( P = \frac{1}{3} \frac{m N \overline{v^2}}{V} \)

∴ \( P = \frac{1}{3} \rho \overline{v^2} \) [∴ \( \frac{m N}{V} = \rho \)]

Q7 Obtain Expression for Kinetic Energy

Ans: Kinetic Energy

\( P = \frac{m N \overline{v^2}}{3V} \Rightarrow PV = \frac{1}{3} M \overline{v^2} \) [ \( M = m N\)]

∴ \( PV = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{2} M \overline{v^2} \)

[ \( E = \) kinetic energy = \( \frac{1}{2} M \overline{v^2} \)]

∴ \( PV = \frac{2}{3} E \Rightarrow E = \frac{3}{2} PV \)

\( E = \frac{3}{2} N k_B T \) [∴ \( PV = N k_B T \) ideal gas equation]

\( \frac{E}{N} = \frac{3}{2} k_B T \) this is kinetic energy per unit molecule.

This means that the average energy per molecule is proportional to the absolute temperature \( T \) of the gas.
Q8 Define Degree of Freedom

Ans: Degrees of freedom of a system are defined as the total number of coordinates or independent quantities required to describe the position and configuration of the system completely.

In Diatomic molecule two categories are divided a. rigid rotator b. Non-Rigid vibrating Molecules

For Non rigid vibrating molecule two degree of freedom of vibrations are present

\( KE \) (Translational) = \( \frac{1}{2} M v^2 \)

\( KE \) (rotational) = \( \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2 \)

\( KE \) (vibrational) = \( \frac{1}{2} M u^2 + \frac{1}{2} k r^2 \)

The degrees of freedom of the system is given by \( f = 3N - k \)

For monoatomic gas, \( N = 1 \) and \( k = 0 \) \( f = 3 \times 1 - 0 = 3 \)

Therefore, a monoatomic gas molecule has 3 degrees of freedom.

For diatomic gas, \( N = 2 \) and \( k = 1 \) \( f = 3 \times 2 - 1 = 5 \)

Therefore, a diatomic gas molecule has 5 degrees of freedom.

For polyatomic gas, \( N = 3 \) and \( k = 3 \) \( f = 3 \times 3 - 3 = 6 \)

Therefore, a polyatomic gas molecule has 6 degrees of freedom.
Q9 Explain Specific Heat

Ans: The amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of a unit mass of an object by \( 1^\circ C \) is called the specific heat of that object.

When the temperature of a gas is increased, even a small rise causes considerable change in volume and pressure. Therefore two specific heats are defined for gases, namely specific heat at constant volume \( C_V \) and specific heat at constant pressure \( C_P \).
Q10 Derive an Expression for Mayers Relation

Ans: Mayer’s Relation \( (C_P - C_V = R) \)

Consider 1 mole of an ideal gas that is enclosed in a cylinder by light frictionless air tight piston. Let \( P, V \) and \( T \) be the pressure, volume and temperature respectively of the gas.

If the gas is heated so that its temperature rises by \( dT \), but the volume remains constant. Since, volume of the gas is constant, no work is done in moving the piston.

∴ \( dQ_1 = dE = C_V dT \) …(1)

where \( C_V \) is the molar specific heat of the gas at constant volume.

On the other hand, if the gas is heated to the same temperature at constant pressure, volume of the gas increases by an amount say \( dV \). Since work is done to move piston, the work done to move the piston \( dW = P dV \)

\( dQ_2 = dE + dW = C_P dT \) …(2)

where \( C_P \) is the molar specific heat of the gas at constant pressure.

But \( dE = C_V dT \) from Eq. (1) as the internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on its temperature.

∴ \( C_P dT = C_V dT + dW \)
or, \( (C_P - C_V) dT = P dV \) …(3)

For one mole of gas, \( PV = RT \)

∴ \( P dV = R dT \), since pressure is constant. Substituting in Eq. (3), we get

\( (C_P - C_V) dT = R dT \)

∴ \( C_P - C_V = R \)

This is known as Mayer’s relation between \( C_P \) and \( C_V \).

Note:
The above relation then is modified to \( C_P - C_V = R/J \) where \( J \) is mechanical equivalent of heat.

Also \( C_P = M_0 S_P \) and \( C_V = M_0 S_V \), where \( M_0 \) is the molar mass of the gas and \( S_P \) and \( S_V \) are respective principal specific heats.

\( S_P - S_V = R / M_0 \)

\( M_0 S_P - M_0 S_V = R / J \)

Q14 Define Coefficient of Reflection (r)

Ans: The ratio of amount of radiant heat reflected by the body to the total amount of heat incident on it is called as coefficient of reflection.

\( r = \frac{Q_r}{Q} \)

where \( r \) = coefficient of reflection or reflecting power or reflectivity or reflectance.
Q15 Define Coefficient of Transmission (t)

Ans: The ratio of amount of radiant heat transferred through the body to the total amount of radiant heat incident on it is called as coefficient of transmission.

Where \( t \) = coefficient of transmission or transmitting power or transmissivity or transmittance.

\( t = \frac{Q_t}{Q} \)
Q16 Obtain the relation between a, r & t

Ans: Let \( Q \) be the total radiant heat incident on the body. Let \( Q_a, Q_r \) & \( Q_t \) be the heat absorbed, reflected & transmitted by the body respectively.

The total radiant heat is given by

\( Q = Q_a + Q_r + Q_t \)

Divide the equation by \( Q \), we get

\( \frac{Q}{Q} = \frac{Q_a}{Q} + \frac{Q_r}{Q} + \frac{Q_t}{Q} \)

\( 1 = a + r + t \)

\( a + r + t = 1 \) This is the relation between the coefficient of absorption, coefficient of reflection and coefficient of transmission.
Q17 What is perfect black body?

Q18 Explain the construction and working of Ferry’s black body.

Ans:

i. Ferry’s perfectly blackbody consists of a double walled hollow sphere having tiny hole or aperture, through which radiant heat can enter.

ii. The space between the walls is evacuated and outer surface of the sphere is silvered.

iii. The inner surface of sphere is coated with lampblack.

iv. There is a conical projection on the inner surface of sphere opposite the aperture. The projection ensures that a ray travelling along the axis of the aperture is not incident normally on the surface and is therefore not reflected back along the same path.

A heat ray entering the sphere through the aperture suffers multiple reflections and is almost completely absorbed inside.

Thus, the aperture behaves like a perfect blackbody.

vi. The effective area of perfectly blackbody is equal to the area of the aperture.
Q19 Define Emissive power

Ans: Emissive power of a body at given temperature is defined as the quantity of radiant energy emitted by the body per unit time per unit surface area of the body at that temperature.

Every body radiates energy at all temperatures except at the absolute zero temperature.

\( R = \frac{Q}{A t} \)

\( Q \) = amount of radiant energy emitted, \( A \) = surface area of the body, \( t \) = time for which the body radiates the energy, \( R \) = Emissive power.

Dimensions of emissive power are \( [L^0 M^1 T^{-3}] \) and SI unit is \( J m^{-2} s^{-1} \) or \( W/m^2 \).

Q21 Define coefficient of emission

Ans: coefficient of emission The coefficient of emission or emissivity (\( e \)) of a given surface is the ratio of the emissive power \( R \) of the surface to the emissive power \( R_b \) of a perfect black surface, at the same temperature. For a perfect blackbody \( e = 1 \), whereas for a perfect reflector \( e = 0 \).

\( e = \frac{R}{R_b} \)
Q22 State and proof Kirchhoff’s Law of Heat Radiation

Ans: Kirchhoff’s Law of Heat Radiation and its Theoretical Proof:

It states that at a given temperature, the ratio of emissive power to coefficient of absorption of a body is equal to the emissive power of a perfect blackbody at the same temperature for all wavelengths.

Symbolically, \( a = e \) or more specifically \( a(\lambda) = e(\lambda) \).

\( R_b \) = emissive power of blackbody \( B \)
\( R \) = emissive power of body \( A \)
\( a \) = coefficient of absorption of body \( A \)

If \( Q \) is the quantity of radiant heat incident on each body in unit time and \( Q_a \) is the quantity of radiant heat absorbed by the body \( A \), then \( Q_a = a Q \)

As the temperatures of the body \( A \) and blackbody \( B \) remain the same, both must emit the same amount as they absorb in unit time.

Quantity of radiant heat absorbed by body \( A \) = Quantity of heat emitted by body \( A \)

\( a Q = R \) …(1)

For Blackbody \( Q = R_b \) (as \( a = 1 \)) …(2)

∴ \( a = \frac{R}{R_b} \) [By dividing eqn 1 and 2]

But \( e = \frac{R}{R_b} \)

∴ \( a = e \)

Alternative Statement:
If a body has high emissive power, it also has high absorptive power and If a body has low emissive power, it also has low absorptive power.
Q23 Explain the Spectral distribution of Black body radiation

Ans: Spectral distribution of Black body radiation

Lumber and Pringsheim stated energy distribution spectrum of black body in terms of wave length. Their experimental result is shown in follow graph. Each curve represents the intensity of radiation against the wave length (\( \lambda \)) for a given temperature of the source.

[Graph showing Radiant power vs wavelength with curves for 1500 K, 1800 K, 2000 K, 2500 K, 3000 K]

From experimental curves, it is observed that

at a given temperature, the energy is not uniformly distributed in the spectrum (i.e., as a function of wavelength) of blackbody.

at a given temperature, the radiant power emitted initially increases with increase of wavelength, reaches its maximum and then decreases. The wavelength corresponding to the radiation of maximum intensity, \( \lambda_{max} \) is characteristic of the temperature of the radiating body. (Remember, it is not the maximum wavelength emitted by the object), area under the curve represents total energy emitted per unit time per unit area by the blackbody at all wavelengths.

as the temperature increases, the peak of the curves shifts towards the left – shorter wavelengths, i.e., the value of \( \lambda_{max} \) decreases with increase in temperature.

at higher temperatures, the radiant power, or total energy emitted per unit time per unit area (i.e., the area under the curve) corresponding to all the wavelengths increases,

at a temperature of 300 K (around room temperature), the most intense of these waves has a wavelength of about \( 5 \times 10^{-6} \) m; the radiant power is smaller for wavelengths different from this value. Practically all the radiant energy at this temperature is carried by waves longer than those corresponding to red light. These are infrared radiations.

Chapter 1: Rotational Dynamics

📖 Core Definitions & Concepts

1. Circular Motion
The motion of an object along the circumference of a circle is called circular motion.

Examples: merry-go-round, vehicle taking a turn, satellite around a planet.
2. Characteristics of Circular Motion
• It is accelerated motion because the direction of velocity continuously changes.
• It is periodic motion because the particle repeats the same path.
3. Radius Vector
A vector describing the position of a particle performing circular motion with respect to the centre of the circle.
4. Angular Displacement
The angle described by the radius vector in a given time at the centre of the circle.

SI unit: radian
It is dimensionless.
θ = s / r
s = rθ
5. Angular Velocity
Angular velocity is the rate of change of angular displacement with respect to time.

SI unit: rad/s
It is a vector quantity.
ω = Δθ / Δt

v = rω
6. Angular Acceleration
Angular acceleration is the rate of change of angular velocity with respect to time.

SI unit: rad/s²
α = Δω / Δt
7. Uniform Circular Motion
Motion of a particle along the circumference of a circle with constant speed.
8. Non-Uniform Circular Motion
Motion of a particle along the circumference of a circle with variable speed.

Centripetal and Centrifugal Force

9. Centripetal Force
The force acting on a particle performing circular motion and directed towards the centre is called centripetal force.
ac = v²/r = rω² = vω

Fc = mv²/r = mrω² = mvω
10. Centrifugal Force
The apparent force acting away from the centre during circular motion is called centrifugal force. It is a pseudo force.

Its magnitude is equal to the centripetal force.

Vehicle on Horizontal Circular Track

For a vehicle moving on a level circular road, static friction provides the centripetal force.
mv²/r = μmg

vmax = √(μrg)

Banking of Road

Banking of road means raising the outer edge of a road above its inner edge through a certain angle.
v = √(rg tanθ)

θ = tan⁻¹(v²/rg)

Moment of Inertia

Moment of inertia is the sum of the products of the mass of each particle and the square of its perpendicular distance from the axis of rotation.
I = Σmᵢrᵢ²

I = ∫r²dm
SI unit: kg m²
Dimensions: [ML²T⁰]

Angular Momentum

L = Iω

Torque

τ = Iα

Rotational Kinetic Energy

K = ½Iω²

Conservation of Angular Momentum

If the net external torque acting on a body is zero, its angular momentum remains constant.
dL/dt = τ

If τ = 0, then L = constant.

Radius of Gyration

I = MK²

K = √(I/M)

Rolling Motion

KEroll = ½Mv² + ½Iω²

ω = v/R

Rotational Kinematic Equations

ω = ω₀ + αt

θ = ω₀t + ½αt²

ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ

Chapter 2: Mechanical Properties of Fluids

HSC Physics | Maharashtra Board

Fluid

A fluid is a substance that can flow. Liquids and gases are common examples.

Ideal Fluid

  • Incompressible
  • Non-viscous
  • Irrotational
  • Steady flow

Pressure

Pressure is the normal force acting per unit area.

P = F / A

SI unit: Pascal (Pa)

Dimensions: [ML⁻¹T⁻²]

Pressure Due to Liquid Column

P = hρg

Here h is depth, ρ is density and g is acceleration due to gravity.

Pascal's Law

Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally and undiminished in all directions.

Applications

  • Hydraulic lift
  • Hydraulic brakes

Atmospheric Pressure

Atmospheric pressure is the pressure produced by the weight of the Earth's atmosphere.

Gauge Pressure

Gauge Pressure = Absolute Pressure − Atmospheric Pressure

Surface Tension

Surface tension is the tangential force acting per unit length on the free surface of a liquid.

T = F / L

SI unit: N/m

Surface Energy

Surface energy is the work required to increase the surface area of a liquid surface.

Angle of Contact

It is the angle between the tangent to the liquid surface and the solid surface at the point of contact.

Laplace's Law

For a liquid drop:

ΔP = 2T / r

For a soap bubble:

ΔP = 4T / r

Capillarity

The rise or fall of a liquid in a narrow tube is called capillarity.

Examples

  • Oil rising through a lamp wick
  • Water movement through blotting paper
  • Water movement through soil
  • Rise of water in plants

Capillary Rise

h = 2T cosθ / (rρg)

Hydrodynamics

Hydrodynamics is the study of fluids in motion.

Streamline Flow

In streamline flow, fluid particles move smoothly in an orderly manner.

Turbulent Flow

Turbulent flow is irregular flow in which velocity can change with time and eddies may form.

Reynolds Number

Rₑ = ρvd / η

Viscosity

Viscosity is the property of a fluid that opposes relative motion between its layers.

Newton's Law of Viscosity

F = ηA(dv/dx)

SI unit of coefficient of viscosity: Pa·s

Stokes' Law

Fᵥ = 6πηrv

Terminal Velocity

The constant velocity reached by a falling body when the forces acting on it become balanced is called terminal velocity.

vₜ = 2r²(ρ − σ)g / 9η

Equation of Continuity

A₁v₁ = A₂v₂
Av = Constant

Important Formulas

P = F/A

P = hρg

T = F/L

ΔP = 2T/r

ΔP = 4T/r

h = 2T cosθ/(rρg)

Rₑ = ρvd/η

Fᵥ = 6πηrv

vₜ = 2r²(ρ−σ)g/(9η)
