3. KINETIC THEORY OF GASES AND RADIATION

Q1. What are the modes of transfer of heat energy?

Ans: Heat energy flows from one point to another due to temperature difference. It moves from a region of higher temperature to a region of lower temperature.

There are three modes of transfer of heat:

1. Conduction
2. Convection
3. Radiation

Conduction: It is the mode of transfer of heat through a material medium without actual migration of the particles of the medium from one place to another. In solids, heat is mainly transferred by conduction. A material medium is necessary.

Convection: It is the mode of transfer of heat through a material medium by the actual migration of particles of the medium. In fluids (liquids and gases), heat is transferred mainly by convection.

Radiation: In radiation, heat is transferred from a body at higher temperature to a body at lower temperature in the form of electromagnetic waves. No material medium is necessary for radiation. Therefore, radiation can travel through vacuum.

Q2. Explain the concept of an ideal gas

Ans: An ideal gas is a gas which fulfils the following conditions:

1. The size of gas molecules is negligibly small.

2. Gas molecules are widely separated and intermolecular forces of attraction are negligible.

3. An ideal gas strictly obeys all gas laws.

Boyle's Law:
P ∝ 1/V   at constant T
Charles' Law:
V ∝ T   at constant P
Gay-Lussac's Law:
P ∝ T   at constant V
The relation between pressure, volume and temperature is:
PV = nRT
Where:
P = pressure of gas
V = volume of gas
n = number of moles
R = gas constant
T = temperature of gas

Q3. What is Equation of State?

Ans: For a gas, its state is specified by physical quantities such as pressure P, temperature T, volume V and internal energy E.

The equation relating these physical quantities is known as the equation of state.

Q4. Write the assumptions of kinetic theory of gases

Ans: 1. Gas consists of a large number of extremely small particles called molecules.

2. All molecules of the same gas are identical.

3. Molecules are rigid and perfectly elastic spheres.

4. Gas molecules are in random motion.

5. Molecules continuously collide with one another and with the walls of the container.

6. These collisions are perfectly elastic.

7. A molecule travels in a straight line with uniform speed between two successive collisions.

8. Collision time is very small compared with the time between two successive collisions.

9. The average number of molecules per unit volume is constant.

10. Intermolecular forces are negligible.

11. Actual volume occupied by molecules is very small compared with the volume of the gas.

12. Average kinetic energy of gas molecules is directly proportional to absolute temperature.

Mean Free Path: The average distance travelled by a molecule between two successive collisions is called mean free path.
λ = 1 / [√2 π d² (N/V)]
Average mean free path:
λ = (λ₁ + λ₂ + λ₃ + ... + λN) / N

Q5. Derive an expression for average pressure of gas using kinetic theory

Ans: Consider an ideal gas enclosed in a cubical vessel of side L.

Suppose a molecule of mass m moves with velocity vₓ towards one wall.

Change in momentum:
Δp = -mvₓ - mvₓ
Δp = -2mvₓ
Momentum transferred to wall:
2mvₓ
Time between two collisions:
Δt = 2L / vₓ
Average force:
F = mvₓ² / L
For N molecules:
F = m/L (vₓ₁² + vₓ₂² + ...)
Pressure is:
P = F/A
Since area A = L²:
P = mN v̄ₓ² / V
Due to random motion:
v̄ₓ² = 1/3 v̄²
Therefore:
P = 1/3 (mN/V) v̄²
Since:
ρ = mN/V
Therefore:
P = 1/3 ρv̄²

Q6. Obtain Root Mean Square Velocity Expression

Ans: We know:
P = mN v̄² / 3V
Therefore:
v̄² = 3PV / mN
Hence RMS velocity:
vrms = √(3PV / mN)
Using ideal gas equation:
vrms = √(3RT / M)
Where:
R = gas constant
T = absolute temperature
M = molar mass

Q7. Obtain expression for Kinetic Energy

Ans: From kinetic theory:
PV = 1/3 Mv̄²
Kinetic energy:
E = 1/2 Mv̄²
Therefore:
PV = 2/3 E
Hence:
E = 3/2 PV
Using:
PV = NkBT
Therefore:
E = 3/2 NkBT
Kinetic energy per molecule:
E/N = 3/2 kBT
Thus average kinetic energy per molecule is directly proportional to absolute temperature.

Q8. Define Degree of Freedom

Ans: Degrees of freedom of a system are defined as the total number of independent coordinates required to completely describe the position and configuration of the system.

Translational kinetic energy:
K.E. = 1/2 Mv²
Rotational kinetic energy:
K.E. = 1/2 Iω²
For a monoatomic gas:
f = 3
For a diatomic gas at ordinary temperatures:
f = 5
For a polyatomic molecule, the degrees of freedom depend on its structure and active modes of motion.

Q9. Explain Specific Heat

Ans: The amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of unit mass of an object by 1°C is called specific heat.

For gases, two specific heats are defined:

1. CV = Specific heat at constant volume

2. CP = Specific heat at constant pressure

Q10. Derive an expression for Mayer's Relation

Ans: For one mole of an ideal gas:

At constant volume:
dQ₁ = dE = CVdT
At constant pressure:
dQ₂ = dE + dW
Therefore:
CPdT = CVdT + PdV
Hence:
(CP - CV)dT = PdV
For one mole:
PV = RT
At constant pressure:
PdV = RdT
Therefore:
CP - CV = R
This is called Mayer's relation.

Q11. Define Diathermanous Body

Ans: A body which allows heat radiation to pass through it is called a diathermanous body.

Q12. Define Athermanous Substance

Ans: A body through which heat radiation cannot pass is called an athermanous body or substance.

Such substances are opaque to heat radiation.

Q13. Define Coefficient of Absorption

Ans: The ratio of the total radiant heat absorbed by a body to the total radiant heat incident on it is called coefficient of absorption.
a = Qa / Q

Q14. Define Coefficient of Reflection

Ans: The ratio of the amount of radiant heat reflected by a body to the total radiant heat incident on it is called coefficient of reflection.
r = Qr / Q

Q15. Define Coefficient of Transmission

Ans: The ratio of the amount of radiant heat transmitted through a body to the total radiant heat incident on it is called coefficient of transmission.
t = Qt / Q

Q16. Obtain the relation between a, r and t

Ans: Let Q be the total radiant heat incident on a body. Let:

Qa = absorbed heat
Qr = reflected heat
Qt = transmitted heat

Therefore:
Q = Qa + Qr + Qt
Dividing by Q:
1 = a + r + t
Hence:
a + r + t = 1

Q17. What is a Perfect Black Body?

Ans: A body which absorbs all the radiant energy incident upon it is called a perfect black body.
a = 1
For a perfect black body:
r = 0,   t = 0

Q18. Explain construction and working of Ferry's black body

Ans: 1. Ferry's black body consists of a double-walled hollow sphere having a small aperture.

2. The space between the walls is evacuated and the outer surface is silvered.

3. The inner surface is coated with lampblack.

4. A conical projection is provided opposite the aperture.

5. Radiation entering through the aperture undergoes multiple reflections.

6. Almost all the radiation is absorbed inside the sphere.

Thus, the aperture behaves like a perfect black body.

Q19. Define Emissive Power

Ans: Emissive power of a body at a given temperature is defined as the quantity of radiant energy emitted by the body per unit time per unit surface area.
R = Q / At
Every body radiates energy at all temperatures except absolute zero temperature.
